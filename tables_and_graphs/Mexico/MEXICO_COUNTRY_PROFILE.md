# MEXICO TRADE FINANCE COUNTRY PROFILE
## January 2022 - August 2025

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Data Coverage:** 2,204 monthly observations
**Period:** January 2022 - August 2025 (44 months)
**Bank Coverage:** 53 financial institutions
**Data Type:** Letters of Credit (LC) ONLY
**Data Source:** Comisión Nacional Bancaria y de Valores (CNBV) - Report R12A Section 133

**Key Limitation:** Mexico's dataset covers **ONLY letters of credit**, not the full trade finance spectrum (pre/post-export financing, trade loans, guarantees). This represents approximately 15-25% of total trade finance activity, making it an incomplete picture of Mexico's TF market.

**Key Insight:** Despite limited scope, the data reveals extreme concentration (CR5=81.0%) and provides baseline for LC market analysis. Mexico's LC portfolio averaged USD 5.25 billion annually (2022-2024), representing only **2.1%** of trade volumes.

1. **Strong corporate treasuries:** OXXO, Cemex, Bimbo, América Móvil finance trade internally

3. **Maquiladora structure:** Assembly plants financed by US/Asian principals
4. **Intercompany trade:** 40-50% of Mexico trade is intra-firm (no external TF needed)

## 9. KEY INSIGHTS & RECOMMENDATIONS

### 9.1 Strengths
✅ **USMCA integration** enables low-cost trade finance (open account)
✅ **Strong corporate treasuries** reduce bank TF dependence
✅ **Nearshoring momentum** (FDI +19.3% in 2023-2024)
✅ **Competitive exchange rate** (MXN competitive for exports)
✅ **Clean CNBV data** (high quality, publicly available)

### 9.2 Critical Challenges
⚠️ **Extreme data gap:** 75-85% of TF market not captured
⚠️ **Highest concentration:** CR5=81.0% (duopoly BBVA + Santander = 51%)
⚠️ **No firm size data:** Cannot assess SME access
⚠️ **No sectoral data:** Cannot analyze auto, electronics, agriculture separately
⚠️ **Foreign bank dominance:** 68.4% (risk of capital flight)
⚠️ **Low LC usage:** 0.5% of trade (vs 15-25% benchmark)

### 9.3 Data Recommendations

**Immediate Actions:**

1. **Expand CNBV Reporting (Priority #1)**
   - Mandate monthly reporting of:
     - Pre-export financing
     - Post-export financing
     - Import/export loans
     - Trade guarantees
   - Add fields: Firm size, sector (SCIAN code), LC type (import/export)
   - Target: Full TF coverage by 2026

2. **Benchmark Study**
   - Survey top 20 banks on full TF portfolio (not just LC)
   - Estimate total market size: USD 25-35 billion hypothesis validation
   - Identify data gaps and reporting barriers

3. **SME Access Assessment**
   - Mandate firm size reporting (aligned with INEGI classification)
   - Survey SME exporters on TF access barriers
   - Target: Establish SME TF baseline by end-2025

### 9.4 Policy Recommendations

**Market Development:**

1. **De-concentration Measures**
   - Encourage regional development banks (Bancomext) to increase TF
   - Attract new foreign TF specialists (Standard Chartered, Mizuho)
   - Target: Reduce CR5 from 81.0% to 65% by 2028

2. **SME Trade Finance Program**
   - Create USD 2 billion Bancomext guarantee fund for SME TF
   - Subsidize LC fees for small exporters (<USD 5M annual revenue)
   - Digital platform for SME import/export documentation

3. **Nearshoring TF Initiative**
   - Co-finance TF for nearshoring-linked exports (auto parts, electronics components)
   - Partner with US Ex-Im Bank and Canadian EDC for bilateral TF
   - Target: Capture USD 3-5 billion of nearshoring TF by 2027

4. **LC Promotion Campaign**
   - Educate exporters (especially to China, India, non-USMCA) on LC benefits
   - Reduce bank LC fees by 20-30% (regulatory negotiation)
   - Target: Increase LC/Trade from 0.5% to 1.5% by 2027

5. **Sectoral Focus:**
   - **Agriculture:** Promote LC for avocado, berry, tequila exports (high-value, smaller firms)
   - **Manufacturing:** Support machinery, pharmaceutical, medical equipment imports from Asia (LC-suitable)
   - **Energy:** Structured LC for LNG, petroleum product imports

## APPENDIX: TECHNICAL DEFINITIONS

### A.1 Letter of Credit (LC)

**Definition:** A payment instrument issued by a bank guaranteeing payment to an exporter upon presentation of compliant shipping documents.

**Types (not distinguished in CNBV data):**
- **Sight LC:** Payment on presentation
- **Usance LC:** Payment after 30/60/90 days
- **Confirmed LC:** Guaranteed by second bank
- **Standby LC:** Guarantee (only paid if default)

### A.2 CNBV Bank Code

**Format:** 5-digit code (XXXXX)
- First digit: Bank type (4=commercial, 6=development)
- Next 4 digits: Sequential institution number

**Example:** 40012 = BBVA México (commercial bank #12)

### A.3 Data ETL Process

See [Scripts/mexico_lc_etl.R](../Scripts/mexico_lc_etl.R) for:
- Bank code standardization (53 institutions)
- Currency conversion (MXN to USD using SAT FIX monthly rate)
- BACI trade data merge (Mexico code 484)
- Aggregate calculation (eliminates cod_inst=5, which is total)

### A.4 Exchange Rate Source

**SAT FIX:** Official MXN/USD exchange rate published by:
- **Servicio de Administración Tributaria (SAT)**
- Used for tax calculations
- Monthly average of daily rates
- Source: [mxnusd.csv](../pre-data/Mexico/mxnusd.csv)

## CONCLUSION

Mexico's trade finance landscape remains a **"black box"** due to severe data limitations. The CNBV captures only **15-26%** of the TF market (letters of credit), leaving USD 20-30 billion of TF activity unobserved.

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Data crisis:** Urgent need to expand CNBV reporting to cover full TF spectrum
2. **Oligopoly:** CR5=81.0% concentration poses systemic risk
3. **Nearshoring moment:** USD 2.3 billion TF opportunity by 2027 if seized
4. **USMCA advantage:** 66% US trade enables low-cost open account, reducing TF need
5. **LC underdevelopment:** 0.5% LC/Trade ratio (vs 15-25% benchmark)

**Priority Recommendation:** Mexico must **urgently expand trade finance data collection** to enable evidence-based policymaking. Without comprehensive data, the country cannot design effective SME support, assess financial stability risks, or capitalize on nearshoring opportunities.
