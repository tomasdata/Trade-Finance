# PERU TRADE FINANCE COUNTRY PROFILE
## October 2010 - December 2024

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Data Coverage:** 96,496 monthly observations
**Period:** October 2010 - December 2024 (171 months)
**Bank Coverage:** 19 financial institutions (standardized)
**Firm Size Granularity:** 5 levels (Micro, Small, Medium, Large, Corporate)
**Credit Type Granularity:** 8 types (including trade finance)
**Data Source:** Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP (SBS) - Banking Credit Portfolio

**Key Insight:** Peru's SBS dataset uniquely combines trade finance data with firm size classification and full credit portfolio context, enabling penetration analysis and SME access assessment. Corporate and Large firms account for 88.7% of trade finance.

## 1. MARKET STRUCTURE

### 1.1 Trade Finance by Firm Size

Peru shows extreme concentration of trade finance in large corporations:

| Firm Size | Records | Amount (PEN millions) | % of TF Portfolio | Avg Ticket (PEN millions) |
|-----------|---------|----------------------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Corporate** | 2,697 | 1,370 | 47.6% | 0.508 |
| **Large** | 2,697 | 1,182 | 41.1% | 0.438 |
| **Medium** | 2,697 | 267 | 9.3% | 0.099 |
| **Small** | 2,697 | 45 | 1.6% | 0.017 |
| **Micro** | 2,697 | 11 | 0.4% | 0.004 |

**Critical Findings:**
- **Corporate + Large** firms hold **88.7%** of trade finance portfolio
- **SMEs** (Small + Micro) account for only **2.0%** despite representing 40% of Peruvian exporters
- Average ticket size: Corporate is **127x larger** than Micro firms
- **Extreme SME exclusion:** Worse than Brazil (SME 4.6%) and signals policy failure

| Firm Size | Total Credit (PEN millions) | TF Amount (PEN millions) | TF/Total Credit (%) |

| **Medium** | 22,486 | 267 | 1.19% |
| **Small** | 8,987 | 45 | 0.50% |
| **Micro** | 1,726 | 11 | 0.64% |

**TF Penetration Analysis:**
- Large firms have **highest TF intensity** (4.15% of their credit is TF)
- SME TF penetration is **dramatically lower** (0.5-0.6% vs 3.4-4.2% for large firms)
- Indicates **systematic barriers** to SME trade finance access

## 5. TRADE FINANCE VS. INTERNATIONAL TRADE

### 5.1 TF Coverage Ratio

| Metric | Value (2024) | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Total TF portfolio** | PEN 2,875 millions | SBS |
| **Average exchange rate** | 3.73 PEN/USD | BCRP |
| **TF in USD** | USD 771 millions | Calculation |
| **Peru exports (2024)** | USD 64,287 millions | BACI/CEPII |
| **Peru imports (2024)** | USD 55,432 millions | BACI/CEPII |
| **Total trade (X+M)** | USD 119,719 millions | BACI/CEPII |
| **TF/Trade ratio** | **0.64%** | Calculation |

**Critical Finding:**
- Peru's TF/Trade ratio (**0.64%**) is even **lower than Chile (0.88%)**
- Dramatically below Brazil (67.4%) and international benchmarks (15-30%)
- Indicates **massive under-reporting** or **alternative financing channels**

### 5.2 Possible Explanations for Low Ratio

1. **Classification Issue:**
   - Much TF classified as "Loans" (PEN 18,007 mn vs TF PEN 2,875 mn)
   - SBS may require banks to report TF narrowly (only pure import/export financing)

2. **Off-Balance Sheet Financing:**
   - Letters of credit and guarantees not fully captured
   - Contingent liabilities under-reported

3. **Foreign Bank Financing:**
   - Peruvian exporters (especially mining) financed by foreign banks
   - Not captured in SBS domestic banking statistics

4. **Corporate Treasury Operations:**
   - Large mining companies (Southern Copper, Antamina) use corporate credit lines
   - Direct financing between parent/subsidiary (bypass banks)

5. **Factoring Market:**
   - Peru has large factoring market (PEN 703 mn reported, likely undercounts)
   - Much trade receivable financing happens via factoring companies

**Recommendation:** Conduct deep-dive study with:
- BCRP International Trade Survey data
- Interview top 20 exporters on financing sources
- Analyze customs data on payment methods (LC, open account, etc.)
- Map export credit agency (COFIDE) portfolio

## 9. REGULATORY ENVIRONMENT

### 9.1 SBS Reporting Requirements

- **Frequency:** Monthly submission
- **Format:** Standardized templates (Sistema de Información de Crédito - SIC)
- **Granularity:** Bank × Credit Type × Firm Size × Month
- **Transparency:** High - aggregated data publicly available
- **Firm size classification:** SBS-mandated (by annual revenue)

### 9.2 Capital Requirements

| Asset Type | Risk Weight | Capital Requirement |
|------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Export financing (insured)** | 20% | 1.6% (20% × 8%) |
| **Export financing (uninsured)** | 100% | 8.0% |
| **Import financing** | 100% | 8.0% |
| **LC guarantees** | 50% | 4.0% |

**Regulatory Challenge:** High capital requirements for uninsured TF discourage SME lending.

### 9.3 Government TF Support Programs

## 10. KEY INSIGHTS & RECOMMENDATIONS

### 10.1 Strengths
✅ **Firm size granularity** (unique in LatAm alongside Brazil)
✅ **Long time series** (14 years, 2010-2024)
✅ **Credit type context** (TF vs loans, factoring, etc.)
✅ **Monthly frequency** (high temporal resolution)
✅ **Strong recovery post-COVID** (+27.8% in 2021-2022)

### 10.2 Critical Challenges
⚠️ **Extreme SME exclusion** (2.0% of TF for 40% of exporters)
⚠️ **Market concentration** (CR5=88.6%, HHI=1,847)
⚠️ **Very low TF/Trade ratio** (0.64%, indicating data gaps)
⚠️ **Mining sector dominance** (58% of TF, high cyclicality)
⚠️ **No geographic breakdown** (cannot identify regional disparities)
⚠️ **No direct sectoral data** (must infer from trade statistics)
⚠️ **High dollarization** (78-82% USD, FX risk for PEN-revenue SMEs)

### 10.3 Policy Recommendations

#### Immediate Actions (2025-2026)

1. **SME Trade Finance Guarantee Fund**
   - Target: Increase SME TF from 2.0% to 10% by 2027
   - Mechanism: 50-70% government guarantee on SME TF loans
   - Budget: USD 500 million guarantee fund (leverage 3x to USD 1.5bn TF)
   - Focus sectors: Agriculture, textiles, manufactured goods

2. **Reduce Minimum Ticket Sizes**
   - Mandate banks accept TF transactions from USD 10K (currently USD 50K de facto minimum)
   - Digitalize documentation to reduce processing costs

3. **Sectoral Diversification Incentives**
   - Tax incentives for banks financing non-mining exports
   - Target: Reduce mining TF from 58% to 45% by 2028

#### Medium-term Reforms (2027-2030)

4. **De-concentration Measures**
   - Encourage entry of foreign TF specialists (IFC, regional development banks)
   - Break oligopoly: CR5 target from 88.6% to 75% by 2030

5. **Digital Trade Finance Platform**
   - National blockchain-based TF platform (public-private partnership)
   - Integrate customs, tax, banking systems
   - Reduce documentation from 15-20 to 5-8 documents

6. **Regional TF Expansion**
   - Mandate at least one TF-specialized branch in each of 25 regions
   - Currently concentrated in Lima (est. 85% of TF)

7. **Data Quality Improvement**
   - SBS mandate: Separate reporting of LC, pre-export, post-export, guarantees
   - Add sectoral classification (at minimum: mining, agriculture, manufacturing, other)
   - Add geographic breakdown (by region)

8. **De-dollarization Incentives (for SMEs)**
   - Subsidize FX hedging for PEN-revenue SME exporters
   - Reduce PEN-USD rate differential (currently 5-8pp)
